\begin{abstract}
We present \modelname{}, an end-to-end study of natural-language-to-shell
models trained from scratch on a CPU-only budget, culminating in a
9.6M-parameter decoder trained on 100M tokens derived from tldr-pages.
Because we control the data generator, we hold out slot values, request
phrasings, and entire command pages independently, pair every neural result
with a BM25 retrieval baseline, and report page-clustered confidence
intervals. Five findings emerge. (1)~Naive sequence packing teaches a
cross-example copying shortcut that validation loss hides; evaluating the
frozen checkpoint under a block-diagonal mask lowers loss by $0.56$ nats,
exposing it without retraining. (2)~At 2.1M parameters, command-family
selection is label-space limited ($0.36\!\to\!0.87$ when 3{,}651 first tokens
are restricted to 111) while argument composition is capacity limited.
(3)~Scaling $4.6\times$ in parameters and $10.9\times$ in tokens lifts
seen-command exact match to $0.525$--$0.559$ but leaves unseen-page exact
match at $0.005$ and \emph{worsens} unseen-page loss ($3.80\!\to\!4.15$).
(4)~Against retrieval, the model's unseen-page token-F1 advantage is
significant on only one of three unseen splits, while on seen pages the two
systems' errors are complementary (oracle routing: $0.914$ vs.\ $0.843$ exact).
(5)~Opening the trained network with unit masks, we find that attention in
layers 0--2 reads the request and early heads copy its entities, that MLPs
at every depth hold command knowledge, and that unseen-page predictions stall
mid-network. Post-training pruning exposes a small-model regime unlike the
LLM one: \emph{no} scoring rule beats random for MLP neurons (10\% removal
already costs 5--9 exact points), whereas Fisher-scored heads with global
allocation can be cut by 25\% for 7 points; and seen-page knowledge is not
localized---removing the units most specific to seen pages is no more
selective than random. Short masked distillation recovers only part of the
loss, and after it the scoring rule no longer matters; but a subnetwork scored
and distilled on ten DevOps command families yields a 2.95M-parameter
specialist that keeps 65\% of the generalist's exact match on those families
($0.300$ vs.\ $0.465$) and selects the family more accurately ($0.91$ vs.\
$0.79$).
We release the full pipeline, probing and pruning harness, checkpoints, and
per-example logs as a reproducible testbed for small-model research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mapping an English request to a single shell command is a bounded, checkable,
and practically useful instance of semantic parsing: outputs are short (median
26 characters), correctness is measurable without human judges, and the
resource a model must possess---a vocabulary of tools and flags---is small
enough to study exhaustively. Prior work established the task with curated
corpora and neural semantic parsers \citep{lin2017tellina,lin2018nl2bash};
modern code LLMs solve it broadly but opaquely at billions of parameters
\citep{chen2021codex,roziere2023codellama}. What is missing is a \emph{small,
fully instrumented} testbed in which every design decision---corpus
construction, tokenization, packing, objective, split design, baselines, and
the internal structure of the trained network---is visible, ablatable, and
honest about failure. \modelname{} (\cref{fig:pipeline}) is such a testbed.
Because we control the data generator, we can hold out slot values, request
phrasings, and entire command pages independently, and because the model is
small (2.1M--9.6M parameters, trained on CPU), we can afford exhaustive
interventions on it.

We organize the study around five research questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\RQ{\arabic*}]
\item \emph{Training signal.} Can a standard efficiency recipe silently change
  the conditional distribution a model learns, and can this be diagnosed
  without retraining? (\cref{sec:shortcut})
\item \emph{Failure decomposition.} Is the residual failure of a tiny model
  limited by the size of the label space or by capacity? (\cref{sec:core})
\item \emph{Scaling.} What does $4.6\times$ parameters and $10.9\times$ tokens
  buy, and along which generalization axis? (\cref{sec:scaling})
\item \emph{Retrieval control.} Does the generative model outperform nearest-
  neighbour retrieval anywhere, under clustered significance testing?
  (\cref{sec:retrieval})
\item \emph{Internal structure.} Where in the network is the command family
  decided and the arguments composed; how much of it can be removed after
  training; and are page-specific memories localized enough to be pruned
  selectively? (\cref{sec:inside})
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A shortcut diagnosis with an eval-time causal test} (\RQ{1}):
  naive sequence packing teaches cross-example copying that validation loss
  conceals; a frozen-checkpoint mask ablation ($3.866\!\to\!3.306$ nats) and a
  behavioural prefix probe identify it without retraining.
\item \textbf{A label-space/capacity decomposition} (\RQ{2}): restricting
  $3{,}651$ first tokens to $111$ raises family selection from $0.36$ to
  $0.87$ at fixed architecture, while argument composition stays the
  bottleneck.
\item \textbf{A scaling result along orthogonal axes} (\RQ{3}): seen-command
  exact match rises from $\leq\!0.002$ to $0.525$--$0.559$, unseen-page exact
  match stays at $0.005$, and unseen-page loss \emph{regresses}
  ($3.80\!\to\!4.15$).
\item \textbf{A significance-tested retrieval accounting} (\RQ{4}): with
  page-clustered paired bootstrap intervals, the generative model's token-F1
  advantage over BM25 on unseen pages is significant on one of three unseen
  splits only; on seen splits, the two systems' errors are complementary
  (an oracle router would reach $0.914$ exact vs.\ $0.843$ for BM25).
\item \textbf{A mechanistic and pruning study in the small-relative-to-data
  regime} (\RQ{5}): early attention reads and copies, MLPs at every depth store
  command knowledge; no MLP scoring rule beats random (10\% removal costs 5--9
  exact points) while Fisher-scored heads with global allocation can be cut by
  25\% for 7 points; page knowledge is not localized; and a family-scored,
  distilled 2.95M subnetwork keeps 65\% of the dense model's exact match on its
  families.
\item \textbf{A released platform}: data and model cards, pinned corpus
  commit, per-run metadata, checkpoints, raw per-example predictions, the
  probing/pruning harness, and a 16-chapter learning book.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{NL-to-shell.} \citet{lin2017tellina} built Tellina, an RNN semantic
parser with slot filling for bash one-liners, and \citet{lin2018nl2bash}
released NL2Bash ($\sim$9{,}305 expert-described commands). \modelname{}
differs in source (curated tldr-pages), scale (from-scratch 2--10M parameters
on CPU), and focus (generalization audit, baselines, and mechanism rather than
leaderboard performance).

\textbf{Small and instruction-tuned code models.} Codex \citep{chen2021codex},
Code Llama \citep{roziere2023codellama}, the Llama family
\citep{touvron2023llama,grattafiori2024llama3}, and instruction tuning
\citep{ouyang2022instructgpt} solve shell questions broadly; we operate three
orders of magnitude smaller to keep every mechanism measurable, in the spirit
of TinyStories \citep{eldan2023tinystories}, TinyLlama
\citep{zhang2024tinyllama}, and nanoGPT \citep{karpathy2023nanogpt}.

\textbf{Sequence packing.} Packing variable-length samples into fixed rows is
standard \citep{krell2021packing,dao2022flashattention}; block-diagonal
masking with position reset restores equivalence to unpacked training
\citep{krell2021packing} and is used in conditioned generation
\citep{copet2023musicgen}. Our contribution is diagnostic: the contamination
left by naive packing is a learned conditional that silently changes the task,
measurable by evaluating a frozen checkpoint under both masks.

\textbf{Scaling and data mixture.} Scaling analyses
\citep{kaplan2020scaling,hoffmann2022chinchilla} concern tokens and parameters
for a fixed distribution; we report a regime in which more tokens (more
phrasings of known pages) improve interpolation and regress extrapolation.
Retrieval augmentation \citep{lewis2020rag,borgeaud2022retro} and execution
environments \citep{yang2023intercode} frame our proposed remedies.

\textbf{Pruning.} Saliency-based pruning dates to Optimal Brain Damage
\citep{lecun1990obd}; magnitude pruning with retraining
\citep{han2015learning} and the lottery-ticket hypothesis
\citep{frankle2019lottery} established that trained networks contain sparse
sub-networks. First-order Taylor saliencies \citep{molchanov2017pruning} and
mask-gradient head importance \citep{michel2019sixteen} showed that many
attention heads can be removed, and that the important ones are specialized
\citep{voita2019heads}; \citet{kwon2022fast} use the Fisher information of
unit masks for retraining-free structured pruning. At LLM scale, SparseGPT
\citep{frantar2023sparsegpt} and Wanda \citep{sun2024wanda} prune weights
one-shot, LLM-Pruner \citep{ma2023llmpruner}, SliceGPT
\citep{ashkboos2024slicegpt}, Sheared LLaMA \citep{xia2024sheared}, and
Minitron \citep{muralidharan2024minitron} remove structure and recover with
continued training or distillation \citep{hinton2015distilling}, and whole
deeper layers are often redundant \citep{men2024shortgpt,gromov2024deeper}.
\citet{hooker2019forget} showed that compression disproportionately harms
rare examples. This literature studies heavily over-parameterized models; we
ask what changes when a model is \emph{small relative to its data}.

\textbf{Interpretability and memorization.} The logit lens
\citep{nostalgebraist2020logitlens} and tuned lens \citep{belrose2023tunedlens}
decode intermediate residual streams; circuit analyses
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2023ioi} identify copying and induction
mechanisms \citep{olsson2022induction}; feed-forward layers act as key--value
memories \citep{geva2021kv} that store factual associations
\citep{dai2022knowledge,meng2022rome}. Memorization is necessary for long-tail
learning \citep{feldman2020memorization}, and whether it is localized in a few
units is contested \citep{chang2024localization,stoehr2024paragraph}. Our
seen-vs-unseen differential pruning is a direct test of localization in a
setting where ``memorized'' (seen pages) and ``novel'' (unseen pages) are
defined by construction.

\section{Task and Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Corpus}
The sole knowledge source is tldr-pages \citep{tldrpages}, pinned at commit
\texttt{95062b79} (\texttt{pages/common} + \texttt{pages/linux};
CC-BY-4.0): 6,803 pages / 30,308 raw examples yield 6,621 usable pages /
28,659 usable base examples after filtering. Each example is a one-line
description plus a command template with \texttt{\{\{placeholder\}\}} slots.
Filters reject multi-statement, substitution-heavy, history-expanded,
interactive, and oversized commands; the run-3 revision additionally rejects
cron-shaped first tokens, leading \texttt{!}, and process substitution
($-139$ examples). Slots are filled from hand-written pools (file, path, user,
host, package, port, pattern, version, and fixed synthetic credentials) with
per-variant fresh values and tldr alternative-syntax resolution.

\subsection{Requests, styles, and splits}
Each base example is rendered into requests by 16 templates (``styles''): 12
train styles (0--7, 12--15) and styles 8--11 \emph{held out} for evaluation.
Slot mentions in the description are substituted inline; remaining slots are
declared in an appended context sentence (``Assume the file is
\texttt{/tmp/notes.txt}.''). Splits are by \emph{page} (deterministic hash of
the page title, 80/10/10), so all examples of a command fall in exactly one
split. Six evaluation splits of 1{,}500 requests each orthogonalize three
axes (\cref{tab:splits}).

\subsection{Versions}
Dataset v1 (runs 1--2, core): 6 variants per base example, 135,860 training
requests, 9.16M packed tokens per epoch. Dataset XL (run 3): 150 variants per
example with 4 additional train styles, 1,303,083 requests, 99,992,950 packed
training tokens in 468,212 document-aligned chunks (17,411,259 supervised
tokens; longest example 229 tokens). Slot-free examples saturate at 12
distinct phrasings; slot-bearing examples scale near-linearly. The same page
split is preserved across versions. Risk labels (129{,}177 safe / 14{,}822
caution / 861 destructive) are regex heuristics used for analysis and CLI
warnings, never as training targets; \texttt{bash -n} validity of a
2{,}000-command sample is $99.85\%$.

\section{Model, Objective, and Training}
\label{sec:model}

\modelname{} is a pre-norm decoder-only transformer
\citep{vaswani2017attention,touvron2023llama}: RMSNorm \citep{zhang2019rmsnorm},
RoPE \citep{su2021roformer}, fused scaled-dot-product attention
\citep{dao2022flashattention}, SwiGLU MLPs \citep{shazeer2020glu}, and tied
embeddings \citep{press2017tying} (\cref{tab:arch}). A byte-level BPE
\citep{sennrich2016bpe} with a 4{,}096 vocabulary and a fixed
system/user/assistant template is shared by all runs; prompt tokens are
masked with $-100$, so the loss covers only the command and EOS.

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}

\midrule

Context / vocab & 256 / 4{,}096 & 256 / 4{,}096 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Architectures. Head dimension is fixed at 32 across scales. In the
9.6M model, MLPs hold 62\% of all parameters, which makes MLP neurons the
natural unit for structured pruning (\cref{sec:inside}).}
\label{tab:arch}
\end{table}

\textbf{Objective.} Token cross-entropy on supervised positions. An optional
\emph{first-token weight} $w$ multiplies the loss on the first command token
(the command-family decision); $w{=}5$ in the core diagnostic, $w{=}1$
elsewhere. We report this decomposition throughout: \emph{first-token} loss
measures family selection, \emph{argument} loss measures composition.

\textbf{Training.} AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} (decay $0.1$ on matrices
only, $\beta{=}(0.9,0.95)$), lr $3\mathrm{e}{-4}$ cosine-annealed to
$3\mathrm{e}{-5}$ after 200--300 warmup steps, gradient clipping at $1.0$,
fp32 on 4 vCPU ARM64 (Neoverse-N1). Examples are packed complete-only into
256-token rows ($\sim$14\% padding). Best checkpoints are selected on
\texttt{val}. Seed 42 throughout; per-run config, environment (Python 3.12,
torch 2.14), git commit, and tokenizer snapshots are stored with the logs.

\section{Evaluation Protocol and Statistics}
\label{sec:protocol}

Greedy decoding, 64 new tokens maximum. \emph{Exact}: normalized prediction
equals the reference. \emph{Command}: first executable token matches
(wrappers such as \texttt{sudo} skipped). \emph{Token F1}: multiset F1 over
arguments. \emph{Valid bash}: \texttt{bash -n} parses (syntax only; nothing
is executed). The BM25 baseline \citep{robertson2009bm25} returns the command
of the most similar training request.

\textbf{Uncertainty.} Proportions carry Wilson 95\% intervals
\citep{wilson1927probable}; at $n{=}1{,}500$ the half-width is at most 2.5
points. Model-vs-BM25 differences are paired (same requests) and are reported
with 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals \citep{efron1993bootstrap} with
$10^4$ resamples. Because all examples of a page share a command, examples are
not independent; we therefore resample \emph{pages} (595--1{,}318 clusters
per split) rather than examples. All runs are single-seed; we flag this as a
threat to validity in \cref{sec:limitations}.

\section{The Packing Shortcut and Its Repair (RQ1)}
\label{sec:shortcut}

\textbf{Run 1 (naive packing).} Concatenating all examples into one stream and
slicing it into 256-token rows lets position $t$ of example $N$ attend to the
\emph{command of example $N{-}1$}. The model reached a plausible loss (best
val $3.9749$) but $0.000$ exact match, with $72\%$ of predictions beginning
with \texttt{sudo} (references: $6\%$), e.g.\ \emph{``list all files
including hidden ones''} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{sudo aa-cli -{}-list}.

\textbf{Diagnosis without retraining.} Two probes on the frozen checkpoint:
(i) a \emph{prefix probe}---prepending an unrelated prior example flips the
prediction to copy it; (ii) a \emph{mask ablation}---evaluating the same
packed validation set with a block-diagonal document mask lowers validation
loss from $3.866$ to $3.306$ ($-0.56$ nats). If preceding examples were useful
context, masking them would \emph{hurt}; the improvement shows a harmful
learned dependence. Both probes are possible because document ids are a
runtime input.

\textbf{Run 2 (document-aligned packing).} Retraining with complete-example
packing, per-token document ids, within-example positions, and the
block-diagonal causal mask \citep{krell2021packing}---same architecture, data,
and optimizer---lowers loss by more than a nat (\cref{tab:run12}).

\begin{finding}{RQ1}
Naive packing changed the learned conditional from
$P(\text{cmd}\mid\text{request})$ to $P(\text{cmd}\mid\text{request},
\text{previous example})$. Validation loss did not reveal this; generation
probes and a frozen-checkpoint mask ablation did. The repair is standard, but
the diagnostic generalizes: \emph{evaluate a frozen model under the
inference-time attention pattern before trusting its training loss.}
\end{finding}

\section{Label Space vs.\ Capacity (RQ2)}
\label{sec:core}

We build a focused subset---pages of $\sim$100 curated common tools: 655
pages, 17{,}317 requests, \textbf{111 distinct first command tokens}
(vs.\ 3{,}651 broad)---and train the identical 2.1M model for 20 epochs with
dropout $0.1$ and first-token weight $5.0$ (2.2\,h, \cref{tab:core}).

At 2.1M parameters, family selection is \emph{label-space limited} and

but it redirected the run-3 budget toward capacity and data rather than
architecture.
\end{finding}

BM25 is near-oracle on seen splits by construction: evaluation requests are
generated paraphrases of training requests, so the nearest training request
usually carries the reference command. On unseen pages it is near-useless
($\leq 0.8\%$ exact) because the correct command is not in its index.
\Cref{tab:bm25} shows two things that point estimates alone hide.

First, the generative model's token-F1 advantage on unseen pages is
\emph{statistically supported only on \texttt{val\_unseen}}
($+0.029$, \ci{$+0.012$}{$+0.046$}); on both test-unseen splits the interval
includes zero. An earlier version of this analysis read the three positive
point estimates as consistent evidence of composition; with clustered
intervals, the evidence is suggestive, not established.

Second, the two systems' errors on seen pages are \emph{complementary}: on
\texttt{test\_seen} the model is exactly right on 106 requests that BM25 gets
wrong. An oracle that picks the right system per request would reach
$(1265+106)/1500 = 0.914$ exact, versus $0.843$ for BM25 and $0.525$ for the
model. This bounds what a confidence-based router (\cref{sec:future}) could
gain.

\begin{finding}{RQ4}
Retrieval dominates seen pages, neither system works on unseen pages, and the
generative model's edge on unseen-page token F1 is real on one of three
splits. Clustered resampling matters: page-level dependence widens intervals
and changes which claims survive.
\end{finding}

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Synthetic evaluation.} Requests are template paraphrases of a curated
corpus (tldr common/linux only) with slot values from small pools;
real-user performance is likely lower and slot robustness may partly reflect
pool memorization. BM25's seen-split strength is an artifact of this design.
\textbf{Metrics.} Exact match grants no semantic equivalence,
\texttt{bash -n} checks syntax only, risk labels are heuristics, and nothing
is executed. \textbf{Single seed.} Every training run uses seed 42; run-to-run
variance is unmeasured, so small differences between runs (as opposed to
within-run paired comparisons, which our bootstrap covers) should be read as
directional. \textbf{Confounded scaling.} Run 3 changes parameters, tokens,
filters, and train styles at once; it is not a controlled ablation of run 2.
\textbf{Core diagnostic.} The core run's first-token weighting tagged only
the first active token per packed chunk (about one example in three), so its
reported effect is a lower bound. \textbf{Pruning study.} Scores and masks are
computed from 512 calibration examples and evaluated on 500 (loss) and 200
(generation) examples per split; generation metrics on 200 examples have
Wilson half-widths up to $\pm7$ points. All pruning results come from one
checkpoint, recovery uses a small budget (400 steps), and latency is measured
with the unbatched reference decoder. \textbf{Safety.} The model
hallucinates flags, cannot verify local tool versions, and must not be given
execution authority; the CLI never runs generated commands.

\section{Future Work: Directions for Small Models}
\label{sec:future}

The pruning results suggest research directions that are specific to small
models, where every parameter is in use.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parser, not library.} If MLP capacity is spent storing a long
  tail of page knowledge that is distributed and cannot be pruned
  selectively, move that knowledge out of the weights. Train the same model
  with the retrieved tldr page of the target tool in context
  \citep{lewis2020rag,borgeaud2022retro} and test two predictions: unseen-page
  exact match rises, and the retrieval-conditioned model tolerates far more
  MLP pruning than \cref{tab:prune} shows, because it no longer needs to
  store flags.
\item \textbf{Generalist-to-specialist distribution.} \Cref{tab:family}
  suggests shipping one generalist and carving per-user specialists on device
  from that user's command history. Open questions: how few calibration
  requests are needed, whether specialists can be merged, and how specialist
  quality scales with the number of families kept.
\item \textbf{Non-uniform architectures from probes.} Heads matter in layers
  0--2 and MLPs everywhere; at a fixed parameter count, a model with full
  attention early, few heads late, and the saved parameters moved into MLP
  width is a direct, cheap architecture test. Late attention layers are also
  candidates for weight sharing \citep{dehghani2019universal}.
\item \textbf{Train-time capacity reallocation.} Dynamic sparse training
  \citep{evci2020rigl} could prune and regrow units during training, testing
  whether the ``no free neurons'' state is a property of the data or of dense
  training.
\item \textbf{Calibration-aware routing.} Pruning lowers unseen-page loss by
  softening confidently wrong predictions; sequence log-probability or
  temperature-scaled confidence could route requests between model and
  retrieval, bounded above by the $+7.1$-point oracle gain of
  \cref{sec:retrieval}, and could trigger abstention on unseen tools.
\item \textbf{Coverage-controlled data mix}: at fixed 100M tokens, trade
  variants per page for more pages, testing whether the unseen-loss
  regression (\cref{tab:losses}) is a mixing artifact.
\item \textbf{Execution-grounded training} \citep{yang2023intercode} and
  \textbf{semantic-equivalence evaluation} to target the 16.7\%
  right-family-wrong-arguments rate.
\item \textbf{Multiple seeds} for every headline comparison, and recovery
  budgets beyond 400 steps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

\modelname{} shows that a 10M-parameter model trained in one CPU-day can become
a usable suggestion engine \emph{within} its training distribution, and that
the same evaluation discipline that shows this also shows where it ends:
page-level generalization. Opening the network sharpens that boundary.
The model's MLPs are full of page knowledge, spread thinly enough that no
neuron is free and no small set of neurons is the ``memory'' of a page; its
attention is concentrated in a few early layers; and on unseen pages its
computation simply has nothing to look up. Two design consequences follow
for small language models. Capacity spent on storing a long tail of
commands is capacity a model this size cannot spare, which argues for
moving that knowledge out of the weights and into retrieved context; and a
generalist is a good \emph{source} of specialists, which argues for
distributing one generalist and extracting per-user subnetworks from it.
Both are now testable on the released platform.

\textbf{Scores.} For MLP neuron $i$ of layer $\ell$ with gate, up, and down
weights $W^g, W^u \in \mathbb{R}^{768\times256}$, $W^d \in
\mathbb{R}^{256\times768}$: magnitude $=\|W^g_{i,:}\|\,\|W^u_{i,:}\|\,
\|W^d_{:,i}\|$; activation$\times$weight $=\mathbb{E}_t|h_{t,i}|\,
\|W^d_{:,i}\|$ over non-pad tokens $t$, where $h$ is the SwiGLU hidden state;
Fisher $=\mathbb{E}_x[(\partial\mathcal{L}(x)/\partial m_{\ell,i})^2]$ with
$\mathcal{L}(x)$ the mean supervised-token loss of request $x$. Head scores
are analogous, using the head's value and output-projection slices and the
norm of its output vector. The differential score of a neuron is
$\tilde F^{\text{unseen}}_u - \tilde F^{\text{seen}}_u$ with
$\tilde F = F/\sum F$; the lowest values (most seen-specific) are removed
first.

\textbf{Copy score.} For each supervised position $t$ whose target token also
occurs in the prompt, the attention mass head $h$ places on the matching
prompt positions, averaged over such positions.
